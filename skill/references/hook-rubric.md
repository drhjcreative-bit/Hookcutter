# Hook & watchability rubric

Score every candidate moment on two axes, 0–10.

## HOOK (scroll-stopping opener strength)

Start at 4. Adjust:

+3  Opens a loop the viewer needs closed ("The reason X is not what you think").
+3  Concrete personal stakes ("I lost $40k so you don't have to").
+2  Specific number or timeframe ("3 mistakes", "in 90 days").
+2  Direct callout of the target viewer ("If you run cold email, stop doing this").
+2  Tension / conflict / a claim that provokes disagreement.
+1  Punchy: 4–14 words. Sayable in under ~3 seconds.
+1  High vocal energy / emphasis (or, in b-roll, the loudest/most intense frame).
−2  Setup, context, throat-clearing, "so basically…", "um", slate/intro.
−2  Wordy: >18 words before the payoff lands.
−1  Requires prior context the cold viewer doesn't have.

A moment scoring **≥7 hook** is a valid cold open. Pick the single highest.

## WATCHABILITY (keeps them past the hook)

Start at 4. Adjust:

+3  Delivers a distinct payoff, insight, or turn.
+2  Emotional beat, surprise, or visual/action peak.
+2  Advances the one idea (doesn't repeat an earlier point).
+1  Tight — no dead air at the head or tail.
−2  Redundant with a higher-scoring moment.
−2  Meandering / low information density.

## AUDIT CHECKLIST (score the assembled edit, 0–10)

Weighted blend:

- **Hook (32%)** — Is the cold open your strongest ≥7 hook, in the first frame?
- **Pace (22%)** — Avg on-screen clip near target (≈2.2s ≤30s, ≈3s ≤60s, ≈4.5s
  extended)? Any single clip >1.6× ideal is a flag. Dead air >0.4s is a flag.
- **Duration fit (16%)** — Total within ~12% of the target length?
- **Coverage (15%)** — Enough distinct moments (≥2 for 15s, ≥3 for ≤60s, ≥5 for
  extended)? Not one long clip masquerading as an edit?
- **Payoff (part of hook/coverage)** — Does it end on a button (punchline, loop
  back to the hook, or explicit CTA), not a fade-out?
- **Caption-ready (15%)** — Dialogue footage assumes word-timed burn-in captions.

Below ~7.5/10 → re-plan tighter once (trim long clips to the ideal, drop the
weakest body moment, or swap in a stronger hook) and keep the better result.
Always surface the score and the single highest-impact fix.
